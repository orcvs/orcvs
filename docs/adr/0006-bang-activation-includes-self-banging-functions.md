# Bang activation includes Self-Banging Functions

Placement admission and its relationship to value readiness and overwrite ordering are amended by [ADR 0060](0060-value-inputs-wait-placement-tests-occupancy.md).

Timing, ordering, and Bang-lifetime clauses below are superseded where they conflict with [ADR 0032](0032-schedule-tick-execution-by-dependency.md). Other decisions remain in force.

Bang is the two-Cell Atom `**`, and every Function has one canonical spelling. An ordinary root Expression is inert until Bang activation. A Self-Banging Function is the explicit exception in activation source: at its own Source-order turn it intrinsically receives Bang activation without creating a Source-resident `**`. This preserves one evaluation rule—an active root evaluates—while making autonomous motion visible in the Function spelling rather than in Function case.

For a Source-resident Bang anchored at `(x, y)`, the aligned cardinal root anchors are north `(x, y-1)`, south `(x, y+1)`, west `(x-2, y)`, and east `(x+2, y)`; only a complete root at one of those anchors activates. A Portal may instead deliver Bang directly to one root without overwriting its Function Cells. Activation recursively includes nested Functions. Under [ADR 0032](0032-schedule-tick-execution-by-dependency.md), a `**` standing in the starting Source Snapshot is display left by an earlier result: it is cleared before evaluation and activates nothing. Only a Bang produced during this Tick activates, and it reaches its consumer during that Tick; the display it leaves behind does not activate again on the next one. Invalid operand spellings are neither activation nor cleanup candidates. Direct delivery and intrinsic self-Bang are activation events, not stored glyphs.

Activation scheduling follows one row-major pass. The Interpreter visits each Expression root once at its anchor Position and tests whether it is active and unlocked. A Self-Banging Function receives intrinsic activation at that turn. A Bang delivered during the pass can activate a root whose turn is ahead, but no root whose turn has passed is revisited; multiple Bangs do not make one root evaluate twice. This visit order decides whether a root has a turn. Effects use ADR 0020's producer anchor and emission order, with later Cell effects winning independently under ADR 0004.

The Directional Bang Functions `*^`, `*v`, `*<`, and `*>` emit the matching root-only Self-Banging Function `^^`, `vv`, `<<`, or `>>`. For a producer at `(x, y)`, north emits at `(x, y-1)`, south at `(x,y+1)`, west at `(x-2,y)`, and east at `(x+2,y)`; every emitted Function has a two-Cell Span. The complete initial destination must be empty and inside the Grid or the producer diagnoses and emits nothing. Generated Functions first receive a turn from the next Source Snapshot.

At its turn, a Self-Banging Function tests only Cells newly entered by a one-Cell move. An empty destination plans one validated Portal bundle: spaces over the current Span, followed by its own spelling at the shifted destination. A blocked or out-of-Grid move instead replaces its current Span with `**`. Complete aligned root contact also directly delivers Bang activation; a later root evaluates at its turn, while an earlier root is not revisited. Partial Language Unit contact diagnoses and delivers nothing. Complete non-root contact adds no collision diagnostic. The generated `**` follows ADR 0032's dependency ordering and display lifetime. Direction and cross-Tick state remain visible in Source.

Collisions add no rule of their own: each contender takes its own refusal, and Turn order decides who finds the Cells empty. Two Self-Banging Functions (movers) approaching each other close their gap by two Cells a Tick, so its parity never changes. An odd gap ends with one free Cell: the mover with the earlier Turn enters it, and the other is blocked and replaces its Span with `**` (`>> <<` becomes ` >>**`). An even gap ends flush: each is blocked by the other and both bang (`>><<` becomes `****`). A mover whose Turn comes before that of the mover ahead of it in the same direction is blocked by it and bangs, and the leader then moves on (`>>>>` becomes `** >>`). Three or more converging movers resolve as these cases Turn by Turn (`>>>>  <<` becomes `** >><<`, then `   ****`). A Directional Bang Function contesting a mover keeps its own refusal: with the earlier Turn it emits and the mover bangs, and when the destination is still occupied at its Turn it diagnoses and emits nothing. A mover standing in that destination and moving back into the Directional Bang Function is blocked by it, so the emission is refused whichever Turn comes first and the pair costs no other Function its Tick. A mover blocked by any other Cell takes the same refusal. Both movers are not consumed into one shared Bang: that needs two Turns to depend on each other before either runs, which is a change to the evaluation machine rather than a rule inside it, and it has no meaning for an emission, which never vacates its own Span. The odd gap matches Orca, which resolves the same event by evaluation order.

Halt `*!` belongs in this decision because its effect is on activation itself: it withholds activation from a root rather than moving a value or changing an address. When Halt is active at its turn, it targets only the Expression root whose anchor Position is one row directly south. It locks that root before the later southward turn, so the complete target Expression contributes no effects. Halt does not change the target Source. An empty target is a no-op; an occupied non-root target diagnoses. Orcvs does not revisit a Halt whose turn has passed, and a suppressed Halt does not lock its own target.
